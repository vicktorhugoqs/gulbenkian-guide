The Gulbenkian in 2 hours

A timed route through the Founder's Collection as reopened in July 2026, following the museum's own circuit — Antiquity and the East first, then Europe, Lalique last. Every work named below has a full entry, photo and story in the main guide, which works offline in the galleries. Practical questions (tickets, hours, metro) are in the FAQ.

The trick to a good two-hour visit is asymmetry: walk briskly past whole categories and spend real minutes with few objects. Gulbenkian collected on the principle of "only the best", so the plan below names roughly one to five works per room and assumes you skip the rest without guilt.

The route

Egyptian Art ~10 min

  • Head of King Senusret III — twelve centimetres of obsidian and one of the great portraits of antiquity: a pharaoh shown worn and heavy-lidded instead of eternally young.
  • Wall-relief of Princess Meritites II — from a tomb in the shadow of the Great Pyramid; bought with Howard Carter as agent, months before he opened Tutankhamun's tomb.
  • Torso of King Pedubast — the peak of Egyptian bronze-casting, made in a politically broken era.

Greco-Roman World & Mesopotamia ~10 min

  • Gold medallion from the Aboukir hoard — Caracalla dressed in the imagery of Alexander the Great; formerly J. Pierpont Morgan's.
  • Assyrian relief of a winged genius — from Ashurnasirpal II's palace at Nimrud, the king's cuneiform titles running straight across the carving. Its siblings line the British Museum and the Met.

Islamic East ~20 min — the heart of the collection

  • Kashan lustre mihrab — a prayer niche in cobalt, turquoise and lustre, made under Mongol patronage.
  • Mamluk mosque lamp — the Quran's Light Verse made physical in enamelled glass.
  • Iznik plate with two peacocks — Ottoman ceramics at their most inventive, c. 1575.
  • Anthology of Iskandar Sultan — a Timurid prince's jewel-box manuscript of 1411; give it painting-gallery time.
  • Jade jug of Ulugh Beg — the drinking vessel of Samarqand's astronomer-king, later inscribed by Mughal emperors.

Armenia & the Far East ~10 min

  • Armenian Bible of 1623 — the collection's most personal room: Gulbenkian was an Istanbul Armenian who funded his scattered community all his life.
  • 'Coromandel' lacquer screen — six metres of incised Chinese lacquer, the most architectural thing a European could own from China.

Ivories & illuminated books ~5 min

  • Book of Hours of Margaret of Cleves — one of the finest illuminated books of the years around 1400.

Renaissance & Baroque painting ~15 min

  • Rembrandt, Portrait of an Old Man — pulled from the Hermitage in 1930 when the Soviets quietly sold masterpieces for hard currency; arguably the best painting in Lisbon.
  • Rubens, Portrait of Helena Fourment — his young wife in black satin, once Catherine the Great's.
  • Ghirlandaio, Portrait of a Young Woman — Quattrocento Florence in one face.
  • Rogier van der Weyden fragments — siblings of London's Magdalen Reading.

French 18th-century decorative arts ~10 min

  • Riesener's roll-top desk — made for Louis XVI's sister-in-law at Versailles; the century's privacy technology, one motion locking every paper.
  • Germain's silver-gilt tureens — commissioned by Empress Elizabeth of Russia, sold off by the Soviets in 1930.

18th-century portraits & views ~10 min

  • Houdon's Diana — a life-size marble goddess balanced on one foot, excluded from the Salon as an affront to modesty; ex-Catherine the Great.
  • La Tour's pastel of Duval de l'Épinoy — regularly ranked among the greatest pastels in existence.
  • Guardi's Regatta on the Grand Canal — Venice dissolved into atmosphere.

Romanticism to Impressionism ~15 min

  • Turner, The Wreck of a Transport Ship — catastrophe as a formal principle.
  • Manet, Boy Blowing Bubbles — a vanitas with a modernist deadpan.
  • Renoir, Portrait of Madame Monet and Monet, The Break-Up of the Ice — Impressionism at its birth and in grief.
  • Degas, Self-Portrait ('Degas Saluant') — the observer who declines to be observed.

René Lalique ~10 min — the signature room

  • The 'Dragonfly' corsage ornament — the most famous piece of Art Nouveau jewellery in existence, bought directly from Lalique, a personal friend of fifty years.
  • The 'Serpents' and 'Peacock' ornaments — theatre and pure structure, side by side.

Numismatics — the new room ~5 min

  • Decadrachm of Syracuse — among the most beautiful coins ever struck, from the collection of Sir Arthur Evans. Coins were Gulbenkian's first collection, begun as a schoolboy — the collection's alpha, placed at the circuit's omega.

Only have one hour?

Walk the same circuit but stop only for twelve works:

Senusret III → Assyrian relief → mihrab → Iskandar anthology → Ulugh Beg jug → Rubens → Rembrandt → roll-top desk → Diana → Manet → Dragonfly → Syracuse decadrachm.

That is 5,000 years in sixty minutes, and every stop earns its place.

Before you go

  • 10:00–18:00, until 21:00 on Saturdays; closed Tuesdays — confirm on gulbenkian.pt. Admission is free until 26 July 2026.
  • Metro São Sebastião or Praça de Espanha; the garden between the Museum and the CAM is free and excellent.
  • Load the full guide on Wi-Fi first — it works offline in the galleries and tracks which works you've seen. Free, no sign-up, no data collected.
