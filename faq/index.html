Gulbenkian Museum — visitor FAQ

Straight answers to the questions people actually ask before visiting the Calouste Gulbenkian Museum in Lisbon. Updated for the reopened Founder's Collection (July 2026). For the works themselves, see the full 42-work guide or the 2-hour highlights route.

Is the Calouste Gulbenkian Museum worth visiting?

Yes — it is one of the finest private art collections ever assembled, spanning ancient Egypt to René Lalique in a calm, garden-set building, and it is far less crowded than Lisbon's Belém sights. Calouste Gulbenkian bought roughly 6,000 objects on the principle of "only the best", so the hit rate per room is unusually high.

How long do you need?

Two hours is comfortable for the Founder's Collection highlights; a focused one-hour visit is possible, and art lovers can fill half a day. The circuit is a single loop: Egypt and the Islamic East first, then Europe, ending in the Lalique room. See our timed route.

What are the must-see works?

The obsidian Head of Senusret III, the Assyrian palace relief, the Kashan lustre mihrab, Ulugh Beg's jade jug, Rembrandt's Portrait of an Old Man, Rubens's Helena Fourment, Houdon's Diana, the Manet–Renoir–Monet–Degas wall, and Lalique's Dragonfly corsage ornament — the most famous piece of Art Nouveau jewellery in existence. All 42 highlights are in the main guide, in visit order.

What changed in the 2026 renovation?

After a 16-month renovation the Founder's Collection reopened on 18 July 2026 with restored daylight and garden views in the galleries, renumbered rooms, and a new numismatics room showing far more of Gulbenkian's coin collection — his first love as a collector — than ever before.

When did the Founder's Collection reopen?

On 18 July 2026, in the Foundation's 70th-anniversary year.

How much are tickets? Is it ever free?

Admission is free until 26 July 2026, for the reopening. After that, the all-inclusive ticket (Museum, CAM and temporary exhibitions) is €14. Entry is always free on Sundays from 14:00 and for under-18s, and free to all exhibitions on Saturdays from 18:00 to 21:00 for under-30s. The surrounding Gulbenkian garden is free and open daily. Prices change — checked 20 July 2026, so confirm on the official tickets page.

What are the opening hours? Which day is it closed?

10:00–18:00, and until 21:00 on Saturdays. Closed on Tuesdays, and on 1 January, Easter Sunday, 1 May, 24 and 25 December. Confirm current hours on the official opening-hours page before you go.

How do I get there by metro?

The museum is at Av. de Berna 45A. The nearest metro stations are São Sebastião (blue and red lines) and Praça de Espanha (blue line), both a short walk away.

Do I need to book in advance?

In the busy period after the reopening — and especially while admission is free — booking online via gulbenkian.pt is a sensible precaution, especially at weekends. Outside peak times the museum rarely sells out.

What is the Founder's Collection?

The roughly 6,000-object personal collection of Calouste Gulbenkian (1869–1955), left to the foundation that bears his name. About a thousand works are shown: ancient Egypt, the Greco-Roman world, the Islamic East, Armenia, the Far East, European painting and decorative arts, and 169 pieces by René Lalique.

Who was Calouste Gulbenkian?

An Istanbul-born Armenian businessman known as "Mr Five Percent" for his share of Middle Eastern oil concessions. He collected on the principle of "only the best" — buying from the Soviet Hermitage sales and Rothschild collections, with Howard Carter once acting as his agent — and settled in Lisbon, where his foundation opened the museum in 1969.

Founder's Collection vs CAM — what's the difference?

The Founder's Collection is Gulbenkian's historic collection, antiquity to about 1900. The CAM (Centro de Arte Moderna), a separate building across the same garden, shows modern and contemporary art with a strong Portuguese focus. Distinct museums, separate tickets, one excellent garden between them.

Gulbenkian vs MAAT vs Museu Nacional de Arte Antiga?

For a world-class collection across 5,000 years, choose the Gulbenkian. The Museu Nacional de Arte Antiga is Portugal's national gallery of old masters (and holds Lisbon's other great Germain silver); MAAT in Belém is contemporary art in a striking riverside building. One museum slot in Lisbon and you love art? Gulbenkian is the safest bet.

Can I see it in one hour?

Yes, if you are selective: follow the circuit and stop at about a dozen works. Our route page includes a one-hour express list.

Is it good for a rainy day?

One of the best in Lisbon: entirely indoors, spacious, rarely crowded, with a garden-view café.

Are there guided tours in English?

The museum runs guided tours, including in English; schedules vary, so check the museum's guided tours page. This guide is a free self-guided alternative in circuit order.

Can I take photographs?

Photography without flash has generally been permitted in the permanent collection, but rules can change and some loans are excluded — follow the signage in the galleries.

Are the gardens free?

Yes — the Gulbenkian garden around the museum buildings is open to the public and is one of Lisbon's best green spaces. Worth 20 minutes before or after.
